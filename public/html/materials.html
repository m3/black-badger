Chapter 01

The Materials

Every Black Badger ring starts with a material that has something to say. Here are the six materials James works with — where they come from, what makes them special, and why they matter.

01

Fordite

Detroit, USA · Compressed Auto Paint

Fordite — also called "Detroit agate" — is built-up layers of auto paint from the Ford Rouge Plant in Dearborn, Michigan. Over decades, spray paint hardened into thick, layered blocks. When cut and polished, each piece reveals a cross-section of American automotive color history.

"It smells like industrial optimism," James says. "You're holding fifty years of factory floors, muscle cars, and paint booths in your hand."

"The Fordite adds the soul of old Detroit. The steel gives you the structure. Together they're something neither could be alone."

Fordite Cross-Section
Origin: Detroit, USA Type: Compressed paint layers Colors: Variable by era

Each piece of Fordite is unique. The colors shift depending on which year the paint was applied and which car model was being sprayed. No two rings are alike.

02

Damascus Steel

Hand-forged by Chris Ploof

Damascus is made by folding and forge-welding alternating steels. The result: a patterned metal where every layer tells you about the process. Beautiful, unpredictable, durable.

James works with a maker called Chris Ploof who forges his Damascus. "Chris takes direction well," James notes, "but this steel has its own ideas. That's the beauty — you guide it, the steel decides."

"Chris Ploof forged this Damascus for me. The Fordite adds the soul of old Detroit. Together they make something neither could be alone."

Damascus Pattern
Maker: Chris Ploof Process: Folded & forged Pattern: Unique each time

03 — Signature

Badgerite™

James's Proprietary Lume Compound

This is the one material on this page that exists because of James alone. Badgerite is his own lume compound — hand-mixed, hand-applied, and signature to Black Badger. It glows in the dark. It's the material that makes a Black Badger piece unmistakable.

Twenty years of finding the right mix. Getting the glow right. Getting the adhesion right. Getting the color right. This is the identity material — the thing that says "this came from the Black Badger workshop."

✦ GLOWS IN THE DARK

"Twenty years of finding the right mix. The glow is the signature."

04

Zirconium

Torch-Reactive Metal

Zirconium behaves differently under a torch. It's like painting with fire — the heat creates a patina of colors that shift over time. No two Zirconium finishes are identical.

"It breathes," James says. "It changes. It lives with you. The patina develops based on your body chemistry, your habits, where you wear it. Every ring becomes personal."

Zirc Patina
Reactive to heat Develops patina Unique to wearer

05

Bluetongue

Damasteel · Gothenburg, Sweden

Bluetongue is a Damasteel product — a stainless Damascus with a blue-black oxide finish that creates concentric circle patterns in the steel. Each ring is different. When James sets stones into Bluetongue, the effect is topographic — like a map of another planet.

"Local material too. Damasteel's foundry is minutes from my workshop. The proximity matters — I can collaborate directly with the metallurgists."

Bluetongue Pattern
Damasteel Swedish-made

06

Ceramic

Aerospace-Grade · Matte Black

Zero corrosion. Matte black. Lighter than titanium. Ceramic is the workhorse of the future — and when James pairs it with Badgerite, the contrast is striking: matte darkness with a glow that comes from within.

The James x Norqain collaboration uses ceramic cases with Badgerite lume — the first time the compound has been applied to a watch. "Taking Black Badger materials into a new context. The ceramic gives you the darkness. Badgerite gives you the soul."
