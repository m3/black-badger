Origin

Gothenburg.
Since 2005.

One room. One maker. Twenty years of finding the right materials, the right process, the right reasons to keep going.

James Thompson · Founder

It's been twenty years since James started Black Badger. From fun little objects to a highly innovative contributor to the Swiss watch industry — the journey has been anything but linear.

The workshop is in Gothenburg, Sweden. It's not big. It doesn't need to be. James works with his hands — cutting, grinding, torching, polishing. Every piece that leaves this room has been touched by every tool in it.

Materials come from everywhere: Fordite from Detroit. Bluetongue from Damasteel's foundry minutes away. Damascus hand-forged by a maker in the US. And Badgerite — that's James's own. A lume compound he developed over twenty years of trial and error.

The rings aren't designed in software. They're discovered in the workshop — what happens when you put two materials together, when you heat zirconium just right, when you cut into a block of Fordite and see what's inside.

"I started this company with the aim of creating fun little objects that had no other job than to make us smile. Twenty years later, that's still the brief. If it doesn't make me smile, it doesn't ship."

— James Thompson, Gothenburg

The Process

01

Material Selection

Every ring starts with the material. James sources Fordite from old auto plants, works with Chris Ploof for Damascus, and mixes Badgerite by hand. The material dictates the design.

02

Cutting & Shaping

Bandsaw. Belt sander. Foredom. The rough shape emerges from raw stock. For tungsten — the hardest metal James uses — this is where patience becomes a tool.

03

Heat Treatment

Zirconium under a torch. The colors shift — gold, blue, purple — based on heat and time. James calls it "painting with fire." Each piece is a different painting.

04

Setting & Inlay

Fordite set into Damascus. Badgerite applied by hand into channels cut into the metal. Each inlay is a separate material story layered into the ring.

05

Finishing

Polishing. Sandblasting. The final texture is a choice — mirror, satin, or raw. James often leaves tool marks visible. "The process should be part of the story."

06

Photography & Archive

Every ring gets photographed in-house. No studio. No retouching. The workshop light is the only light. Each piece gets a serial number and enters the archive.

Collaborations

Standing on the shoulders of makers

Norqain

Swiss watch brand. The "Broken Hour" collaboration brought Badgerite to a watch dial for the first time. Chapter 04 of the Black Badger universe.

Chris Ploof

Custom knifemaker. Forges the Damascus steel James uses. "Chris takes direction well, but the steel has its own ideas."

Damasteel

Swedish Damascus manufacturer. Foundry minutes from James's workshop. Bluetongue and RWL-34 — the backbone of the Damascus line.
